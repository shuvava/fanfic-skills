# Part Checks — Self-Review Reference

Seven checks run over each part before showing Layer 2 / 3 to the user. All are `warning`, never `blocking` — a part that breaks a check on purpose is a choice to record, not a defect. For a published part the card is a reference and the checks report only; for an unwritten part a finding is a plan fix, made now.

## The seven checks

### 1. The climax answers the question

A part asks one question the reader waits to find out. The climax answers it. If the part asks «will she find allies» and climaxes in a won duel, the question and the climax disagree.

**Failed when:** the resolution reported in the Layer 2 paragraph is not what the climax scene delivers.

**Fix:** rewrite the question, rewrite the climax, or rewrite the resolution. All three are equally legitimate; the mismatch is the bug.

### 2. The protagonist decides it, on the page

The climax is a scene where the protagonist chooses between the card's two options and acts. Not a result reported afterwards; not a rescue by someone else.

**Failed when:** the resolution is told in summary, or the deciding act is performed by a non-protagonist while the protagonist watches.

**Fix:** add or rewrite the scene where the protagonist chooses. If the resolution cannot be the protagonist's act (e.g. the character is captured or absent), the question was wrong.

### 3. The cost is not "nothing"

The protagonist pays or loses something at the climax. Unless the part is declared a return to where it began (a comic or slice-of-life part may be) — record that as a choice on the card.

**Failed when:** the resolution paragraph says «and then she has what she wanted» or equivalent — a win with no counterweight.

**Fix:** name what the protagonist pays. The card's `Cost` line is the natural place.

### 4. The trouble grows and comes from different sides

Each complication is harder than the one before and has a different source. The opposition presses again in the middle of the part. Three obstacles from one source in a row is one obstacle repeated.

**Failed when:** the same antagonist / system / pressure appears three times in the part without a new element.

**Fix:** vary the source. A guild problem → a family problem → a public-shaming problem beats three guild problems of escalating intensity.

### 5. The part is not the last one, bigger

If two climaxes in the book are «wins a fight, a harder one», the second is a repeat. The reader has already had that payoff.

**Failed when:** the part's climax matches the structural shape of an earlier part's climax, only scaled up.

**Fix:** change the kind of climax. A duel climax should be followed by a political, social, or moral climax, not a harder duel.

### 6. No thread drops silently

Every thread the part's chapters touch is on the card, marked `closed here` or `carried on`. A thread that simply stops is the failure this check catches.

**Failed when:** a thread appears in a chapter of this part but is neither resolved nor carried forward in the card.

**Fix:** either resolve the thread in this part, add it to the card as `carried on`, or remove the chapter that introduced it.

### 7. The reflection is short and ends in a decision

At most one pause chapter follows a climax — a chapter where the protagonist only reacts and nothing new happens on the page. A longer pause after a peak is the advice serial authors give against, and it is where an aftermath turns into filler. Chapters after the climax that carry their own event — a consequence arriving, another thread moving — are not a pause and do not count.

The reflection ends in a decision. One that ends in a mood leaves the next part without a goal.

**Failed when:** more than one chapter after the climax has no new event on the page, OR the chapter(s) after the climax end in a mood without a decision.

**Fix:** either compress the pause to one chapter, add an event to the second chapter, or add a decision at the end of the reflection.

## Worked example

«Хроники Севера», parts II and III:

- Part II climax: Anna wins the trial (scene where she testifies against Mark).
- Part III climax: Mark publicly shames Anna at the council meeting.

Check 5 flags this — two public-ordeal climaxes, second is just a harder version. Fix: change Part III's climax to a private act (e.g. Anna chooses to resign from the council before being expelled), giving the book one public-ordeal climax and one private-decision climax.

## Reporting in the run

Per the body, the part checks run during self-review at each layer. Report what the self-review found rather than silently fixing it. A finding is named with the part number, the check that failed, the chapter or scene it concerns, and the suggested fix. The user decides whether to apply the fix or record the divergence.
