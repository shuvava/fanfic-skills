# Pitfalls — checklists

Every rule here was paid for with a wasted generation. SKILL.md says **when** to run each list;
this file says **what** to check. Each item: the rule, then the failure that taught it. Cite items
by id in iteration logs ("S7: posture") so a round's fix is traceable.

## I — Identity blocks (Phase 2, and any character or element block)

- **I1 Distinguishing features first.** The two or three that identify the person at thumbnail
  size lead; then age and build, face, hair, eyes, skin, signature clothing and prop, posture.
- **I2 Concrete visual words only.** "A thin white scar through the left eyebrow", not
  "battle-worn". No personality adjectives the model cannot draw.
- **I3 No expressions.** A squint, a smirk, "calculating" eyes are per-scene mimicry; in an identity
  block they fight the reference sheet's neutral face and every scene that needs another one.
  Describe shape ("narrow sharp eyes") and put the look into SCENE.
- **I4 Anchor colours the style lightens.** Black eyes under a style that asks for bright
  highlights came out grey three rounds running; "irises as dark as his hair" held.
- **I5 Do not name what should not show.** "Small ears mostly covered by her hair" produced
  prominent ears. Describe what covers it ("hair falling down both sides of her face to the chin").
- **I6 A canon simile is not a material.** "As if made of mother-of-pearl" means iridescent;
  literal mother-of-pearl renders pale. Find the property the line points at (full spectrum,
  shifting, striking across a room) and pick words that carry it ("like polished opal"); anchor a
  feature that must outshine the style ("the most vivid colour in the whole picture").
- **I7 Barely-there canon details stay out of the block** ("a tiny scar") unless the user wants
  them — a model exaggerates whatever it is told to draw. Record them in the facts table.
- **I8 Use the model's own vocabulary.** An anime model knows "sidelocks", "hime cut", "ahoge" from
  its captions; the named structure hid the ears where "hair covering her ears" did not.
- **I9 Minors.** Canon age under 18: hems no shorter than just above the knee; no framing, pose or
  wording that sexualises; no bust emphasis — say so when asked and offer the nearest acceptable
  option. A girl who "reads as a boy" is fixed by cut and build: tailoring (waisted, flared rather
  than boxy), narrow shoulders, small waist, softer face.
- **I10 Clothing names everything a model would guess:** sleeves or sleeveless, open or closed and
  how it fastens, what is worn over what, what shows at neck and cuffs. "Doublet" alone came out
  sleeveless, then as an open jacket with a belt buckled across the gap.
- **I11 A garment shared across sexes shares colours and details, not the cut.**
- **I12 Every unstated delta seen in an image goes into the block** (boots came out laced), so
  chapter scenes stop varying it.
- **I13 A changed draft block changes in every file that pastes it.** Re-run the check with every
  sheet passed; a stale copy fails as "missing".

## W — World and recurring elements (Phase 2b)

- **W1 Look a place up before inventing it.** Read what canon says about a room's furnishing; the
  user's taste fills only what canon leaves open. (The club had no bookshelves — books live in the
  academy's libraries.)
- **W2 Draw what a viewer would see.** The lamp's bands and core are what Mark found *inside* when
  he unscrewed it; forcing them outside turned an elegant globe into a cage.
- **W3 Name the objects, exclude the rest.** "Trophies" in a fantasy room invites mounted monster
  heads canon never described: "silver cups and medals; no monster or animal heads".
- **W4 A creature that appears in a picture gets its own sheet** from its canon description.
- **W5 Wall objects need their mount.** "Glowing orbs on the walls" float; "brass wall sconces,
  each holding a glass globe" hang.
- **W6 A prop words cannot hold gets a reference image** after two failed rounds (orbs floating,
  then modern shaded sconces): the object alone, lit and unlit, front and side, first locked
  character sheet attached as style reference.
- **W7 The world guard is concrete.** "Modern technology" does not stop a phone or a ballpoint pen;
  when one appears, ban it by name in the chapter's AVOID at once and propose a new guard version.
- **W8 Writing uses the world's script.** Say what it is ("small handwritten hieroglyphs") and ban
  only the wrong script ("never Latin letters"); a blanket "no text" fights every notebook, sign and
  exam sheet the scene needs.

## S — Scene composition (Phase 3, before the budget check)

Read the SCENE once per item.

- **S1 Posture for every figure.** Say "seated" or "standing" for each named character and who else
  stands ("only the two teachers stand"). Reach verbs ("pokes his back from the row above", "peers
  over his shoulder") make the model stand the figure up to reach; give the reach a path ("leaning
  forward over her own desk").
- **S2 The setting's rules.** Read the chapter for what the place forbids or requires: in an exam
  where "nobody may stand up", one standing girl is a canon error.
- **S3 Anchor positions to the room, not the camera.** After a camera change "one row behind him"
  put Anna lower and beside Mark; "one tier higher, farther from the blackboard than he is"
  holds from any angle.
- **S4 Name the contact point.** "Her quill tip on his back, not his head" — or a poke lands on the
  temple with a finger.
- **S5 Both hands, one job each.** Say what each hand does; "elbows him" gave a hand sunk into her
  own chest; "left elbow in his arm" *and* "left hand flat on the table" gave three hands. Add
  "each person has exactly two hands" whenever two people touch.
- **S6 Never solve a hand conflict with a second prop.** "Writes with one quill, pokes with
  another" gave two quills; the user wanted one. Drop an action and count held objects: "she holds
  exactly one quill in total".
- **S7 Name every held object** — the quill, not "writing" — or the model supplies the modern
  default (ballpoint pen, phone). Book covers: "a blank cover", or a title appears in English.
- **S8 Tie similar costumes to their wearers.** When blocks differ in a few items the model swaps
  them: "Mark, the boy, wears trousers, never a skirt; Anna, the girl, wears the skirt, never
  trousers".
- **S9 Adults need their own clothing.** Uniform blocks dress the students; an undescribed
  "examiner" arrives in a suit and necktie.
- **S10 Keep the era guard's full block** whenever adults or writing are in frame — its short form
  lost both the neckties and the script rule.
- **S11 Distance is not size.** "Small in the distance" gave an ant-sized examiner. Place a far
  figure by where they stand ("on the floor at the front, far below"), keep "normal human scale",
  size props against a person ("reaching the middle of his chest"), and put props on what canon
  puts them on (the table), or they land on the stairs.
  Size a figure against the crowd's stated age, not against the crowd: "a head taller than everyone
  around him" in ch04 shrank the senior students into children. Give the crowd's age ("sixteen or
  seventeen, taller than Mark") and then the big one relative to them.
- **S12 Frame the key prop.** If the chapter's prop matters, choose a camera that holds it; a shot
  at bench level pushed the examiner's table off-picture.
- **S13 Background people get faces:** "ordinary, simply drawn faces, not resembling <named
  characters>". "Faces not detailed" produced faceless mannequins. Give their
  number and where they sit, or extras appear.
  Blur words in the crowd clause ("slightly out of focus") and trimming AVOID's "faceless or
  blank-faced" to "faceless" blanked every face in ch03's crowd: keep the full phrase, no blur.
- **S14 Guard against duplicates of a named face** in the crowd: "anyone else with Mark's or
  Anna's face" in AVOID — and still look for one when reviewing.
  Hair colour is the usual twin: with a black-haired hero, add "no other black-haired boy" to
  AVOID before round 1 — an edit recolours one lookalike per request and ch04 had two.
- **S15 Planted characters stay unrecognisable.** One planted for a later chapter may appear where
  the text puts them — small, doing what the text says — never in the pose the later chapter pays
  off (the lecturer walking far rows, not standing over Mark's shoulder).
- **S16 Scene state never contradicts an identity block.** A chapter that needs a character to look
  different is scene state (a hood hides the hair) or a new version — never an edited block.
- **S17 Relative height comes from the sheets and a height reference, not the verbs.** "Tilts her
  head to look up into his face" with a low camera drew Mark a head taller. State the sheets'
  relative height ("half a head taller than him"), use a gaze and camera that fit it (upright, eye
  level — a lean costs height). Words alone still lose: each sheet fills its own canvas, so the model
  has no shared scale and falls back on "boy taller than girl". When two locked characters stand
  together, attach `refs/height-<a>-<b>-v<N>.png` as the last image ("height reference only; copy
  their relative height, not the poses") — built locally from the sheets' front views, feet on one
  baseline, scaled to the recorded heights. It took ch03 four rounds to learn this.
  Name who is taller first, then measure by a landmark: "Anna is slightly taller: the top of
  Mark's head is level with her eyebrows". "Half a head" overshot to a full head; the landmark
  alone, with no one named taller, came out reversed. On stairs keep "on the same step", or a step adds another half
  head. Do not ban the opposite in AVOID ("Mark taller than Anna"): it pushes past the target.
- **S18 A held object is gripped, and a paper shows no text.** "His right hand holds one folded
  sheet at his side" left the hand empty and the sheet floating by his thigh, covered in
  pseudo-Latin. Name the grip ("gripping it between thumb and fingers") and which face shows ("only
  its blank back showing") unless the writing itself is the point.

## E — Edits and repairs (after an image comes back)

- **E1 Edit or regenerate?** Edits change details: colour, a boot, a collar, hair over an ear.
  Silhouettes, proportions, scale and placement are composition — regenerate with a new SHOT. An
  edit asked to fix an ant-sized examiner kept him and added a stool.
- **E2 One change per edit request.** Given three, a model makes the most visible one. Chain
  single-change edits, each labelled (```` ```prompt 2c ````) and run with `--block 2c`; a label
  survives blocks inserted above it, a position does not.
- **E3 Chain order:** limbs and furniture → gaze and expression → colour (hair, eyes) last, so later
  edits do not undo colour fixes.
- **E4 Every edit re-renders the whole image.** Unprotected details drift: two edits turned opal eyes
  amber; one turned black eyes blue. Name what must survive ("keep her rainbow opal eyes exactly")
  and compare each pass against the best earlier round.
- **E5 Patch lost details locally.** `magick` crop the region from the good round, feather a mask,
  composite at the re-measured position (edits shift faces a few pixels). Free and exact. When the
  fix and what must survive sit in different regions (crowd faces fixed, the pair drifted), composite
  the pair back from the round before the edit. **Verify the patch**: `magick compare -metric RMSE`
  of the result against both sources must be non-zero — ch03's first mask silently returned r4.
- **E6 Measure the palette at the end of a chain.** Four edits raised ch02's mean saturation 25 %
  with a pink cast. Compare `magick <img> -colorspace HSL -format "%[fx:mean.g]" info:` with the last
  round before the chain; if it rose, offer `magick <in> -modulate 100,<ratio×100>,100 <out>` side
  by side with the original.
  Measure both files in the same format: re-saving as JPEG
  raised measured saturation 0.172 → 0.197 on dark noisy areas — save local fixes as PNG. Seedream drifts faster: each edit added 10–15 % (ch04 r1 0.170 → r4 0.222) — plan the
  `-modulate` fix as the chain's last step, not a surprise.
- **E7 Gaze edits on a small face barely register** ("eyes turn to his sheet" only lowered her
  lids). Say so before running one; if the gaze carries the scene, write it into the SHOT.
- **E8 Identity references: crop to what must stay.** A whole sheet attached to a new sheet prompt
  is copied wholesale — poses, cut, waist. When body or clothing must change, attach only the face
  (`sips -c <h> <w> --cropOffset <y> <x>`), labelled "face and hair only; draw her body, figure and
  clothing fresh".
- **E9 Stuck three rounds on one feature?** Stop adding words — the text is losing to the style or
  the model's habit. Edit the image (the edited image then becomes the reference), accept the
  rendering and record the decision, or — only if every future character benefits — change the
  style block and log it in `STYLE.md`.
- **E10 A round that made it worse is reverted,** not buried under qualifiers.
